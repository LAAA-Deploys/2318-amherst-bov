Executive Summary

Entitled Transit Site
114 apartments approved, about 800 feet from the Expo/Bundy E Line station
City Planning approved an 8-story, 114-unit building on the site under TOC Tier 4 (DIR-2024-179-TOC-VHCA). A buyer starts with a vested approval and a full planning set instead of starting entitlement from scratch.

2318, 2322, 2328 and 2332 S Amherst Avenue are four contiguous lots totaling 25,014 SF (0.57 acres, per survey) in Sawtelle, each improved with a 1940s single-family home that will be delivered vacant. The site is zoned RAS4-1VL under the Exposition Corridor Transit Neighborhood Plan and sits about 0.15 miles from the Expo/Bundy station.

The approved project is an 8-story, 83-foot building with 114 apartments (101 market rate and 13 Extremely Low Income) over two levels of subterranean parking, designed by KFA. We priced the site two ways: as entitled, and re-entitled under the newer programs the site now qualifies for, including Executive Directive 1, the City's Mixed Income Incentive Program and the state density bonus. We recommend a list price of $7,500,000 ($299.83 per land SF, $65,789 per approved unit).

Key Takeaways

1
Unentitled lots on this block trade near $266 per SF. The sale across the street closed at $266 per land SF in November 2025, and the two Wellesley lots behind it at $258 and $268, all as single-family lots with no approval.
2
An approval is worth a premium. Entitled sites nearby sold for $45,000 to $70,000 per unit, but none had more than 60 units. This site offers 114 approved units and a vested application, which saves a buyer the entitlement time and risk.
3
Commercial-zoned sites price higher. The seven sales with commercial or industrial zoning averaged $328 per SF. The subject's RAS4 zoning is a residential-commercial zone under the Expo plan.
4
A 100% affordable buyer can underwrite about twice the units. As an ED 1 eligible site in a Very Low Vehicle Travel Area, the site supports roughly 200 to 240 affordable units, about $34,000 per unit at our recommended price. An ED 1 building is already rising one block north.
5
Measure ULA applies at any realistic price. Above $5,400,000 the City's transfer tax is 4% of the full price; at $7,500,000 that is $300,000, paid by the seller unless negotiated otherwise.

As entitled. Unentitled lots on the subject's block traded at $258 to $268 per SF over the past two years, and the nine sales within 0.8 miles averaged $269 per SF. A buyer here gets an approved 114-unit building, a vested application and a full planning set, so the site should trade above those lots. At $299.83 per SF and $65,789 per approved unit, the recommended price sits about 13% above the block's unentitled sales and within the $45,000 to $70,000 per unit range paid for smaller entitled sites nearby.

Re-entitled. A tax-credit or ED 1 sponsor would underwrite about 200 to 240 units, or roughly $31,000 to $38,000 per unit at $7,500,000. That is below the $46,946 per unit asked for our 149-unit ED 1 site at 2600 S Robertson and the $70,000 per unit paid for 12461 Louise in Mar Vista. That buyer pool supports the price, but it must file and wait for a new approval, so we would not price above the as-entitled range to reach it. The re-entitled view is the reason to expect competition at list, not a reason to ask more.

Final pricing depends on two items we still need: confirmation that all four lots convey (two are held by family trusts) and the approval and expiration dates in the TOC determination letter.
